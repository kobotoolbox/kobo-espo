CSO mapping: who is there, where, doing what

From the Kobo and WHO Draft CSO mapping tool: civil society organisations working on health emergencies register with a Kobo form, then keep their profile current in a CSO portal and report what they are doing in each place. WHO's country coordinators see their country's CSOs, mapped to official P-codes, and only what each CSO agreed to share goes further. Kenya is the pilot; another country gets its own forms or uses one generic form.

  1. Register

    A CSO fills in its profile on a Kobo form: who it is, where it works, what it does, offers and seeks.

  2. Keep current

    Its people sign in to the CSO portal: profile, places and contacts, and updates on what they do where.

  3. Coordinate

    The country's coordinators see every CSO in their country, by P-code, with flags on what to check.

The forms

Two Kobo forms carry the draft's questions with their lists and codes (workflows/cso/lists/cso-lists.json, read by both the forms and EspoCRM, so the two always agree):

  • The CSO profile, the draft's 17 questions: name and type, where it's registered, the levels it works at, its focal points (as many as it has), whether its profile may be shared, where it works, the settings and people it reaches, its stages, roles and hazards, the roles it could take on with support, the coordination it takes part in, what it offers and seeks (at most three each), how it prefers to connect.
  • The CSO update, the draft's 6: which CSO, where, which activities, their status (planned, ready to activate, active, temporarily suspended, completed), whom they reach, and the main gap or need there.

Each comes in two styles, made by kobo/forms/cso.py together with its bridge template, so a form and its template always match:

  • A country's own form (Kenya's): places as a cascade, county then sub-county, from the official boundaries.
  • The generic form, for any other country: the country, then a pin dropped on the map; the place is worked out afterwards (see P-codes).

For a cholera mapping, cso.py --cholera asks the draft's 12 cholera roles in place of the general ones; EspoCRM keeps the two lists in separate fields.

Places and P-codes

Every place ends as an admin area with its P-code, the OCHA codes that let data from different organisations line up on one map. Kenya's are the 47 counties and 290 sub-counties of its COD-AB boundaries (KE047 Nairobi, KE047288 Kamukunji), loaded from the Humanitarian Geocoder (espo/seed/admin_areas.py).

  • From a country's form the place is the sub-county (or county) picked, linked by its P-code.
  • From a pin, the agent CSO: P-code the places asks the geocoder, every hour, which areas the pin falls in, and writes their P-codes; the place is then linked to the deepest area EspoCRM has. A pin outside the country's boundaries is flagged. If the geocoder can't be reached, the pin waits for the next hour.

A place carries the P-code of each level up to its county, its country, and what the dashboards read of its CSO (its type, settings, people reached, roles), so a map of places needs nothing else.

A CSO's profile

A profile is a CSO record, with its code (CSO- and four characters) and every answer of the profile form. With it come:

  • its Account, named after it: what the portal goes by;
  • its contacts, the focal points from the form, one per person (by email: the same person sent again is the same contact; someone who works with two CSOs is a contact of each);
  • its places;
  • its countries: where it's registered, where its places are, and the others it named. They decide its teams (who sees it).

Who sees what

  • A country's coordinators (WHO's country office; role CSO Coordinator, team CSO: Kenya) see and keep every CSO in their country, its places, contacts and updates, whatever it answered about sharing. A CSO in two countries is in both teams. A CSO in no country with a team is in CSO: Unassigned, for Kobo's staff to place.
  • Other countries' coordinators don't see it; nor do the 5W's cluster users.
  • The CSO's own people see their own CSO, in the portal.
  • Other CSOs and partners see what the CSO agreed to, through the dashboards (the directory and the matching), never EspoCRM's screens:
    • Yes: its profile and its contacts (those still its, and shown in the directory);
    • Profile only: its profile, no contacts;
    • No: not listed at all.

The profile's Shared contact is made by formula from those rules, and it's the only contact the directory reads. When a CSO changes its answer, or a contact changes, it follows.

The CSO portal

A CSO registers with the Kobo form once; after that its people keep it current in the CSO portal (EspoCRM's own portal, at /portal/cso). A coordinator gives a CSO's contact a portal login; they get an email to set their password.

Signed in, they see their own CSO only:

  • its profile, which they can change: its type, its lists, whether it may be shared. Its code, the date of its profile and what's worked out for it (its countries, listing, shared contact) they can read but not change; its key they don't see;
  • its places: they add a place (a sub-county), and it's their CSO's, with its P-codes and teams; they remove one it no longer works in;
  • its contacts: as many as it has. Someone who joins is added; someone who leaves is marked no longer a contact of its CSO, and drops out of its shared contact and the directory (contacts aren't deleted, so who was there stays on record). A contact can be kept out of the directory;
  • its updates: they post one, and it counts at once (they signed in).

If the CSO sends the Kobo profile again (from the link in its email, offline perhaps), the places the form gives replace the form's earlier ones; places added in the portal stay, and its contacts are matched by email, never removed.

Updates

An update says what a CSO is doing in one place now. It counts on the maps when it's the CSO's own:

  • made in the portal, or sent with the CSO's key (in the link its email gives it, filled in on the form), it counts;
  • sent with the code only (typed) or a wrong key, it's kept and flagged, but doesn't count until a coordinator sets its key to Confirmed: a guessed code isn't enough to report for a CSO.

The latest update of a CSO in a place is the one that counts; it marks the earlier ones superseded. An update lists everything the CSO is doing there now, so an activity left out of a newer one is no longer counted.

Data checks flag, for the coordinators: no key or a wrong one, a pin outside the country, a place that isn't one of the CSO's, an activity that isn't in its profile. Flags, not refusals: the update is kept, and fixed or explained.

Emails

EspoCRM sends its emails through Resend, from noreply@respond.kobolabs.dev (Running it).

  • The welcome email. A CSO's new focal point, from the Kobo form, gets its CSO's code and its links: the update form, with the code and key filled in (so what they send counts), the profile form to send again, and the portal. It's made when the contact is, and EspoCRM sends it within ten minutes; an email problem never stops a registration. It's on the CSO's Account, for the coordinators to see. Contacts added in the portal don't get it: their CSO's people know it already.
  • Its links are the instance's, in deploy/instance.toml ([params.cso]): each country's forms (a link fills in a form's questions by its Kobo id) and the portal's address. On the test instance only Resend's test inbox gets the welcome email, since the made-up contacts are at example.org.
  • The portal login: a coordinator asks for it in Claude, through the EspoCRM MCP's invite_portal_user (the contact, the portal, its role); EspoCRM emails them a link to set their password. Or an admin: Contacts, the contact, Create Portal User.

Adding a country

  1. Load its admin areas from the geocoder: python3 -I espo/seed/admin_areas.py UG (its ISO2 or ISO3 code; --max-level 2 to stop at districts).
  2. Make its team, CSO: and the country's name as its boundaries have it (e.g. CSO: Uganda), and give its coordinators the role CSO Coordinator.
  3. Either give it forms of its own (add it to COUNTRIES in kobo/forms/cso.py with its levels' names, and rerun it), or share the generic forms with its CSOs.
  4. Rerun the seed. CSOs already registered there move into its team.

A pin anywhere the geocoder has boundaries is P-coded; 110 countries are loaded there.

Limits and open questions

  • The forms need a Kobo login on the test account: for real CSOs, the projects must accept submissions without one.
  • Not built yet: the matching of offers and needs, the four dashboards (the directory, the map of presence, gaps and overlaps, the matching), and follow-up when a profile goes stale. See the plan, plans/cso-mapping-plan.md.
  • Portal logins need an admin in EspoCRM, so a coordinator asks for one through the MCP (invite_portal_user) rather than in EspoCRM's screens.
  • To agree with WHO: the hazards list (the draft has none; a suggested one is in), the list of needs on the update form (it uses the offers list now), splitting the CSO type question, the countries after Kenya, the languages, and where it runs and whose data it is.
  • Kenya stops at sub-counties: the boundaries have no wards.
  • Jamaica's areas, from the Hurricane Melissa form, use other codes than the geocoder's, so CSO mapping in Jamaica waits for a decision on them.
