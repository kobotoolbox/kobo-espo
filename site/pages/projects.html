Cluster projects: several teams in one response

A response such as Hurricane Melissa Response often supports several clusters at once. Each gets a cluster project in the response, with a team of its own. The Emergency Telecommunications team manages its sites, notes and tasks and sees only those; the WASH team sees only theirs. The response's coordinators see every project.

  1. The response

    In a team for its coordinators, who see all of it.

  2. A project per cluster

    Each in its own team and the response's. Its sites and tasks follow it.

  3. Its people

    In the project's team, each with a role: lead, partner or viewer.

Who sees what

A Cluster project belongs to a response and has a cluster, a status and a lead. Its records are in two teams:

  • the project's own team (e.g. Cluster: Hurricane Melissa Response · ETC): its people see the project, its sites and its tasks, and nothing of the other projects;
  • the response's team: its coordinators see every project. Put only coordinators in it.

Above the responses is the country's coordination team, Coordination: Jamaica say. It's made with the first response in that country, and every response there is in it, with everything in those responses. Its people see all of the country's responses, the way a country office does over several emergencies. If a response's country is changed, it moves to that country's team, and so do its projects, sites and tasks.

A site or a task in a project takes the project's teams by itself. If a team is added to the project later (a partner organisation's, say), its sites and tasks get it too. Closing a project gives its tasks back to its lead, as closing a response does.

The project's people can't open the response itself, only their project. In testing (tests/cases/projects.py, 59 checks), each project's people saw only their own project's sites, tasks and task board, and were refused the other project's records by their links.

Leadership and people

As the UNDAC Handbook describes clusters (A.3.2–A.3.4): a cluster is activated for a response when it's needed, and deactivated later. A project's status reads Planning, Activated or Deactivated.

  • Its sector's kind: a cluster (one of the eleven global clusters, Logistics and CCCM among them), an area of responsibility of Protection (Child Protection), a working group (Cash), or coordination. It comes from the sector.
  • Its approach: cluster, where a lead agency leads, or sector, where the government leads and the agency co-leads. In the handbook's words, "the main difference between clusters and sectors lies in their leadership structure".
  • Its lead agency and co-leads: organisations (their Accounts, the same as in the 5W). A new project gets its sector's global lead as a suggestion (fig. A.4: WASH UNICEF, Logistics and ETC WFP, CCCM IOM…), and in the sector approach as a co-lead. Change it to whoever leads in the country: the handbook's "organisation best suited", a line ministry co-chairing, a national NGO co-leading. Where the global lead isn't in the organisations list, the next one is used: for Health, PAHO (WHO's regional office for the Americas) in the Melissa list.
  • Its people: its Cluster contacts, each with a role (coordinator, co-coordinator, information management officer, government focal point) and an organisation, picked from the list or typed in (a ministry). Each is a Contact on that organisation's Account, found by email or phone, or made. The project's team and the response's see them; the cluster's lead adds them. These are for the people who don't sign in. People who sign in are users in the project's team, with the cluster roles below.

Everyone in a project can post notes on its sites and tasks and mention its people. Nobody can delete records or export them.

The response's coordinators have the staff role. They see every project's work but can only give it to people they share a team with, so a coordinator who hands out a project's tasks joins that project's team. Whoever assigns it, a task can only go to someone in its project's or response's teams: the formula on tasks refuses anyone else.

Tasks on sites and cases

A task's Parent can be a site, or a case (as in EspoCRM itself). A site's page has a Tasks panel.

  • A task on a site goes into the site's project and response, and their teams: Fix the generator on the VSAT hub is seen by the ETC team and the coordinators.
  • A task on a case is in the case's team.
  • The people on it must be in those teams, as for any task.

Setting it up from a conversation

The bridge's EspoCRM MCP (see Connecting a form) has the tools for it, and a prompt, Set up a response, with the steps:

  • create_response: the response and its coordinators' team;
  • create_project: a project per cluster, each with a team of its own, its approach, lead agency and co-leads (the global leads suggested when left out);
  • list_roles, invite_user: a person with a role, in a project's team;
  • add_to_team, remove_from_team: someone joins or leaves a project;
  • list_projects: the responses, their projects and teams.

Each change is shown first and made only once you agree, like the connection tools. They make regular users only, never admins, and never use the bridge's or the agents' roles.

Signing in. invite_user never takes or shows a password. EspoCRM emails the new person a link to set their own, through its outgoing email (Resend on the test instance: see Email).

Sites in a project come from Kobo as any site does: the registration connection sets the site's project, and a visit form's site list names the project's team (team = "Cluster: …"), so it lists that project's sites only.

Limits

  • Access is per person, not per project. In EspoCRM someone's access is the sum of their roles, in every team they're in. A person who leads one project and only views another has lead access in both. Give them the role that suits both, or two accounts.
  • Taking someone out of a project's team hides its records from them at once, except tasks assigned to them or shared with them, until those are given to someone else (or the project is closed).
  • A team added to a response, or taken off it, follows to its projects, its sites and tasks outside a project, its 5W records and its assessments. The coordination team follows its country by itself; don't take it off a response by hand, or it stays off until the country is changed.
