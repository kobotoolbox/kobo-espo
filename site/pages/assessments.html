Assessment registry: who is assessing what, where, when

The UNDAC Handbook asks a response's Assessment and Analysis (A&A) Cell to keep a registry of planned, ongoing and finished assessments, so that organisations don't assess the same people twice, and to share coverage maps back. Here, organisations register their assessments with a Kobo form, the A&A Cell reviews them in EspoCRM, overlaps and missing reports are flagged and followed up, and a dashboard shows who covers which parish and where the gaps are. It runs next to the 5W on the response's instance.

  1. Register

    An organisation fills in the form: what, which sectors, where, when, how, and later its report.

  2. Check

    The A&A Cell reviews it; overlaps with other organisations' assessments are flagged.

  3. Share

    The whole response sees the registry and its coverage; a follow-up asks for reports.

The form

One Kobo form, Assessment registration, on the model of OCHA's registry for the 2026 Venezuela earthquakes, with the response's own lists where that one asks free text, so that an organisation is spelled one way:

  • the response, the organisation registering it and its lead (the 5W's organisations list, or a name typed in), the participating organisations, and the person registering (a Contact, by email);
  • the assessment: its title, the sectors it covers (the response's sectors, and Inter-cluster), how it's coordinated (the handbook's three levels: uncoordinated, harmonised, joint), its methodology and how often it's done;
  • when: start and end dates (the end estimated until it's done);
  • where: the level (parish, community, sites, or the whole affected area), then one row per area, picked from the country's admin areas, and localities;
  • its status (planned, ongoing, completed, report shared, cancelled), the report and the dataset (files), a link to where the report is published, and who may see each (public, within the response, on request).

The form and its connection template are written together (kobo/forms/assessment.py), from the same lists EspoCRM uses (workflows/assessments/lists/), so the two always agree.

An assessment

A submission makes an Assessment with a short code (ASM-4P4W), and one Assessment area for each area it covers. An area has its P-codes up to the parish and its country, and copies its assessment's sectors, status and dates, so a map can count assessments by parish in any sector.

The organisation is the 5W's: the assessment is on its organisation's Account, the same one its 5W registrations are on, so an organisation's page shows its assessments next to its activities.

Who sees what

A registry is meant to be shared, so the whole response sees every assessment: it is in the response's team and every cluster project's team in it, not only those of its sectors (a WASH assessment is seen by the Shelter cluster too, which may plan its own in the same place). A cluster project added later gets them all as well.

  • The A&A Cell (the response's coordinators, Internal Staff in the response's team) reads and edits every assessment, and is the only one who can set its review.
  • Cluster leads and partners read them all, register their own, and edit only their own.
  • Cluster viewers read them.
  • People outside the response see none of them.

Review and sending again

A new assessment is Not checked yet. The A&A Cell checks it and marks it Checked, Needs information or Duplicate. It shows on the response's dashboard straight away, marked as not checked; a public view would show checked ones only.

An organisation updates its assessment by sending the form again with the assessment's code (in the link it's given): the same assessment changes, its status with it. Its areas become the new submission's (an area dropped stops counting), and a checked assessment goes back to Not checked yet, as there is something new to look at.

Overlaps and data checks

The handbook's A&A guidance is about harmonising assessments: the same places, at agreed times, with comparable questions. So when an assessment is saved, it's compared with the others in the response. It overlaps another organisation's when they share a sector, an area (the same P-code, or one inside the other: a community is inside its parish) and dates. The later of the two is flagged, naming the earlier one, and the A&A Cell puts the organisations in touch.

The other flags (the Data checks field, never a refusal):

  • Completed 14 days ago, no report: no report file and no link to one.
  • Planned, but its start has passed.
  • Not completed, but its end has passed.
  • No areas given, where it isn't for the whole affected area.

An assessment that ends before it starts is refused, from the form or anywhere else.

Follow-up

Three agents follow the registry up, rules only (no AI model), each making a task for the response's lead, about the assessment, one open task at a time:

  • Assessments: no report, every morning: completed 14 days ago or more, and no report or link. Ask the organisation for it.
  • Assessments: start passed, every morning: still planned after its start date. Ask whether it started, moved or was dropped.
  • Assessments: overlap, when an overlap is found: put the two organisations in touch.

They are set up in dry run on the test instance (what they would do is recorded, nothing made), as every example agent is: see Responses for how agents work.

Dashboard

A kobo-dash dashboard reads the response's assessments and their areas from two EspoCRM feeds, scoped to the response's team (the feeds leave out cancelled assessments, and areas no longer current). Its filters, sector and status, work on every page.

The parishes' boundaries come from the Humanitarian Geocoder, whose P-codes EspoCRM's areas have (the Hurricane Melissa form's own codes are each area's other P-code: see P-codes). The dashboard is built by site/dash-assessments.py from feeds made by site/dash-assessments-feeds.py; the demo data is site/demo-assessments.py.

Limits and open questions

  • Only the later of two overlapping assessments is flagged: the earlier one gets its flag the next time it's saved. The overlap agent's task names both.
  • "The whole affected area" isn't compared for overlaps (it has no areas to compare).
  • An area outside the response's country isn't checked yet: a response's country is free text.
  • A public view (checked assessments only, nothing shared "on request") isn't set up yet.
  • Assessments in the organisation portal come with the portal itself (the 5W plan).
  • The form lists Jamaica's areas (the test response's): another country's response needs its own areas list.
  • Joint assessments are one record of type joint with its participating organisations, for now.
