The 5W: who does what, where, when, for whom

Organisations report their activities through a Kobo form modelled on the Hurricane Melissa 5W form, with its codes. Each report becomes an activity in EspoCRM, in the sector's cluster project, with a record for each community it reached. The information management team follows up there: organisations that haven't reported, and figures that look wrong.

  1. Register

    An organisation, from the list, signs up for the response and the sectors it works in.

  2. Report

    Each activity: who leads and implements it, what, when, and for each community the people reached.

  3. Follow up

    A task when an organisation is silent for a week; flags on figures that don't add up.

The form, and its codes

The activity report is made from the Hurricane Melissa 5W form (version 1.9) by kobo/forms/melissa.py: the same questions, notes, checks and codes, so its submissions fit EspoCRM as the Melissa form's do. Three things change:

  • the response is picked from EspoCRM's list;
  • the organisations, and the parishes and communities, are EspoCRM's lists on the form (organisations.csv, areas.csv), filtered as the Melissa form filters them: organisations by type, communities by parish. The areas are the COD-AB's, with their P-codes, not the Melissa form's own codes: each area keeps the Melissa code as its other P-code (see P-codes);
  • a community can name a registered site, by its site code.

EspoCRM uses the form's own codes: its 13 sectors (EMERGENCY_TELECOMS, WASH, SHELTER…), 84 activity types, statuses, resource types and units, location types and groups of people. A cluster project's sector is one of them.

Organisations

The Organisations list is the Melissa form's 137, each with its ORG_ code and type. Everyone can read it; the IM team keeps it.

  • An organisation not in the list picks Other and types its name. Its report is kept, and the response's lead gets a task: Add to the organisations list: …
  • An organisation can also register for a response with the registration form: the organisation (from the list), the sectors it works in, its focal point and any others. Registering again updates the same record. A registration is in the teams of the response's projects in those sectors, so each cluster sees the organisations working in it.

Activities and communities

A report is an Activity: its lead and implementing organisation (the lead, unless another is named), sector, activity type and title, status, resource type and unit, dates. It goes into the response's project for its sector.

The form's Figures by community section is repeated, once for each community. Each one is an Activity location: the parish and community (by P-code), the site if it's a registered one, the location type, quantity and budget, people targeted and reached, and by group (displaced, hosting, non-displaced, other) the people, women, men, children and households, targeted and reached. The activity keeps the totals of its locations: communities, people, quantity, budget.

An organisation is one Account, whatever the response: its registrations, activities and locations are on it, and its people are its Contacts (several, each active or not). The registration's focal points become Contacts on it, matched by email, so registering again or for another response doesn't duplicate anyone.

The form's focal points are Focal points on the activity: who the report named. Each is linked to its person, a Contact on the organisation's Account, found by email (else phone) or made, so someone named in every monthly report is one Contact, whose page lists every activity they were named in. A report from the field becomes these records within a minute; one with a community EspoCRM doesn't know is kept with the communities it does, and the bridge records which row it couldn't place.

The organisation portal

Organisations register and report in Kobo, offline and in the field. They keep what they reported current in EspoCRM's organisation portal (/portal/organisations), as CSOs do theirs. An organisation's people there can:

  • see its registrations, one per response, and correct its details: acronym, type, sectors. Not the response, its key or its status, which are the coordinators';
  • see, add and correct its activities and their communities. A new one picks its registration (so its response), and gets the organisation, its Account and its sector's cluster project as a report from Kobo would. The data checks run the same, and the coordinators see the change in the activity's stream. An activity in another organisation's registration is refused;
  • keep its people current: add someone, correct them, untick Active for someone who left (kept, and left out of the contact list);
  • read its clusters and who to contact in them: the cluster projects it's registered in, and their coordinators, IMOs and government focal points.

It never shows another organisation: who else works where is the dashboard's.

A coordinator gives someone a login with the EspoCRM MCP's invite_portal_user (a Contact on the organisation's Account; EspoCRM emails them a link to set their password). A newly registered organisation's focal point gets a welcome email: its registration code, the activity report's link with the response and the organisation filled in, and the portal's address. The instance turns it on and gives the links (deploy/instance.toml, [params.fivew]).

Clusters' people, the contact list

A cluster project has its Cluster contacts: its coordinator, co-coordinator, IMO, government focal point (see Cluster projects). Its partners are the organisations registered in its sector: an organisation's registration adds its Account to the partners of its sectors' projects, and takes it off when it drops a sector. The portal follows the partners: an organisation reads its clusters, and their contacts, and no others.

The handbook lists the contact list with the 3W among a response's information products. The EspoCRM MCP's contact_list gives it, for a response or one cluster: each cluster's contacts with their roles, and its partner organisations with their active people, and all of it as a CSV. It's people's contact details: for the coordinators to share with the response, not to publish (no role exports from EspoCRM, and the dashboard doesn't show it).

Who sees what

  • An activity, its locations and focal points are in its sector's project's team, and the response's: the cluster's people see their sector's 5W, the coordinators see all of it.
  • An activity in a sector the response has no project for is in the response's team only.
  • An organisation's Account and its people are in the teams of all its registrations: the coordinators of each response it registered for, and the clusters it's in, see them.
  • Cluster Viewers read activities and locations but not focal points or organisations' people, which are people's contact details.
  • A Cluster Partner can report an activity in their project, and edit their own.
  • An organisation's people, in the organisation portal, see their organisation's records and its clusters only.
  • The ICCG (the clusters' coordinators together) reads the 3W across clusters on the dashboard: in EspoCRM a coordinator works on their own cluster, since a role's team access can't be read-only for some teams and editable for others.

Follow-up: organisations that haven't reported

The agent 5W: no report runs every morning at 06:00 (UTC), without a model. For each registered organisation that hasn't reported an activity for a week, or never has a week after registering, it makes a task for the response's lead: No 5W report from …, about that organisation (on its Tasks panel), with a note on its page.

  • One task at a time: while it's open, the next mornings only note that it still is.
  • Once the task is done, a further week of silence makes a new one.
  • An organisation marked Inactive isn't followed up.

Like every agent, it starts in dry run: switch it to Active on its page (Agents).

Data checks

Activities and their locations carry Data checks: flags on what looks wrong, set as the figures arrive and gone when they're fixed. A report is never refused for them: it's kept, then fixed or explained in a note.

OnFlag
a locationMore reached than targeted
a locationThe groups add up to more than the total reached
a locationA group's women, men and children don't add up to its people
an activityNo community given

Filter the Activities or Activity locations list by Data checks to see what needs a look.

Dashboard: the 5W and the 3W across clusters

The response's dashboard on kobo-dash (see Dashboards) reads two feeds in the response's team, so every cluster: the activities' communities, and the registered organisations. Each community carries its activity's organisation, sector, type and status, and its parish's P-code, so one feed draws the 3W. Its three pages:

  • The 5W: people reached and targeted, communities, organisations; people reached by parish; communities by sector, organisation and activity type; who does what where;
  • 3W across clusters, for the ICCG's gaps and duplication: communities by parish and sector, organisations by sector, a map of the parishes with none of a chosen sector, and the duplication table;
  • Organisations by cluster: the directory of registered organisations, their types and clusters, and whether they report. No one's contact details: that's the contact list.

Duplication: a community where another organisation does an activity of the same sector and type (Other: the same type typed in), neither completed, is flagged with Overlaps with, naming the other organisation and activity. The later report is flagged; the flag is worked out again when the community's record is saved.

Short codes

Responses, projects, registrations, activities, tasks and applications have a short code (RSP-7K3Q, PRJ-…, REG-…, ACT-…): easy to say on a call, found by the list's search box, and the names Kobo lists use for their choices. EspoCRM gives them; they can't be changed. Sites keep their site code, areas their P-code.

Limits

  • The areas list (789 parishes and communities) goes to every phone with the form.
  • An organisation's report isn't checked against its registration: the list is what it picks from, registration is optional.
  • Charts, maps and the 5W's tables are dashboards: see Dashboards.
  • An overlap flags the later community only, and goes when that one is saved again after the other activity is completed.
  • A correction in the portal and a resubmission from Kobo can disagree: the latest save wins, and the stream shows both.
  • The portal uses EspoCRM's own layouts, with the coordinators' fields (keys, teams, Kobo links) hidden.
